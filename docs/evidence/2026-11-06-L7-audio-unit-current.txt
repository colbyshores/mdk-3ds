    2. THE UNIT, and the reason this batch exists. The DSP rate is
       NDSP_SAMPLE_RATE = SYSCLOCK_SOC/512 = 16756991/512 = 32728.5 Hz
       (ndsp.h:9, os.h:9), NOT the 32768 Hz batch 88 printed. With the
       conventional 160-sample frame that predicts 204.55/s; a power-of-two
       32768 Hz predicts 204.80/s. The emulator reads 204.75/s at 19 s and
       204.71/s at 30 s, and secs is printed to 2 dp, so the 30 s figure is
       bounded to (204.671, 204.739] -- which EXCLUDES both candidates. So the
       honest verdict is weaker than batch 88's and weaker than the reviewer's
       open question 1 hoped: this counter is not a rate instrument at all. The
       quantum implied by the header rate at the measured value is 159.88, not
       160, and no integer quantum fits. What the counter does establish is the
       KIND of the thing (one ndspThreadMain iteration per increment, per the
       libctru disassembly) and that the DSP is running at all; the console's
       mixer rate and the DSP frame quantum are both UNMEASURED here, and the
       emulator's own HLE pacing -- with the audio device muted -- is the likeliest
       source of the 0.07% that neither candidate explains.
