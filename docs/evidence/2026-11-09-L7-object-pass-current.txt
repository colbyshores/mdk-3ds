  objects: stream mesh 4096 corners = 80 KB linear (480 corners, 11 draws this frame)

  1. THE LINEAR-MEMORY ANSWER, which is the question CONTINUATION asked rather
     than a glue check. `ObjectDraw::ensure` starts the ONE stream mesh at
     kInitialStreamCapacity = 4096 corners and doubles it to what the frames
     reach. Level 7 reaches 570 corners at steady state, so the mesh is made ONCE
     at 4096 corners and NEVER GROWS: 4096 x 5 floats x 4 B = 81920 B = 80 KB of
     linear memory, against the roughly one megabyte of linear headroom
     `renderer_c3d.cpp`'s residency note budgets after the two eye targets, the
     command buffer and an arena's textures. The C# instead allocates
     `Renderer.StreamVertices` = 1 << 18 vertices = 5.2 MiB and keeps it for the
     process; on this target that is 5.2 MiB out of a heap measured in single
     megabytes, and a failed `linearAlloc` is a silently missing mesh, not an
     error. The geometric growth is therefore doing its job: 80 KB committed,
     11.4% of it used, zero regrowths after frame 2.
     The 4096-vs-570 gap is why the log prints the frame's own corner count and
     not just the capacity: the capacity is rounded up to a power of two, so the
     capacity alone cannot tell a 570-corner level from one that nearly fills its
     4096. Level 8's measured 5616 corners would take it to 8192 = 160 KB, and
     that is still inside the headroom.
  2. THE ABSOLUTE CHECK, and it is not a picture. The oracle golden
     `tests/scripts/golden/L7_t1000_draw.txt` ends `STREAM vertices=570` and
     queues 13 draws over 7 objects. The console logs `obj=570c/13d` at steady
     state. Same level, same object count, same corner count, same draw count —
     so what the console hands its GPU seam is the oracle's stream, and the
     picture difference below is the GPU half doing something with it.
