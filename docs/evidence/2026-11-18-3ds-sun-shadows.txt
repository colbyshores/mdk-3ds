1. WHAT THE PC DOES, AND WHAT THE PICA DOES INSTEAD
---------------------------------------------------
PC (enhanced.frag, 11_shadow_post.inc): the frame's opaque Lit triangles through
sunShadowMatrix (an orthographic view along the sun, 600 world units square
around the camera, 8000 deep) into a 2048x2048 depth map; every lit pixel
compares its depth with the map's (2x2, blended) and the sun's share of its
light is multiplied by the answer.

3DS, two passes, every one of them on the GPU:
- THE CASTER PASS, once a frame, before the eyes (03_frame.inc castShadows).
  The same casters, through the same matrix, into a PICA shadow texture: 512
  texels a side, or 256 when VRAM is short (section 5). Solid casters are culled
  as the PC culls them, which matters (section 3).
- THE RECEIVE PASS, once per eye, after the opaque passes (06_draw.inc
  receiveShadows). Every opaque Lit triangle mesh is drawn again -- same vertex
  shader, depth test EQUAL, nothing written to depth -- with the shadow map on
  texture unit 0 and a second geometry shader (mdk_shadow.g.pica) that works
  out, per triangle, R = F_shadow / F_lit: what the lit program's colour has to
  be multiplied by to become the colour the same surface has without the sun.
  The combiner makes mix(1, R, shadow) and the blend multiplies the picture by
  it. The haze's share of a pixel is left alone, and the shadow fades out over
  the last tenth of the map, as the PC's does.
The self-shadow guards are the PC's scaled to the map's texel: the PC pushes a
receiver 0.5 world units along its normal against a 0.29-unit texel; here that
is 2 units at 512 and 4 at 256 (the same 1.7 texels).

2. HOW THE MAP IS WRITTEN: TWO WAYS, CHOSEN BY MEASURING THE GPU
----------------------------------------------------------------
A shadow texel is (depth24 << 8) | softness; in memory, per texel, the bytes are
[depth high, depth mid, depth low, softness] (both instruments, read back).
The caster pass has two ways to write it, and each instrument fails one:
- NATIVE: GPU_FRAGOPMODE_SHADOW, the PICA's own mode -- the fragment's 24-bit
  depth goes into the map, the nearer kept, combiner green 0 (a hard shadow).
- THE RAMP: an ordinary draw whose vertex shader (mdk_caster.v.pica) hands the
  depth over as texture coordinates, s = depth and t = 256 * depth (REPEAT), into
  a 512x256 NEAREST ramp texture holding the bytes; the map's own 16-bit depth
  buffer keeps the nearer caster. (The 512 columns and the half-step correction:
  pica_matrix.h, THE DEPTH RAMP.)
What was measured:
- EMULATOR: native writes into the real 256x256 caster pass land NOWHERE (0
  texels, read back; the first version of this work found the same with probes,
  history in git). The ramp works.
- CONSOLE: the ramp does NOT: the console does not repeat t = 256 * depth. Its
  map, read back over HTTP and compared byte for byte with the emulator's (level
  8 frame 60): the depth's middle byte 0 in every written texel, the high byte
  rounded to the nearest step -- the depth good to 1/256 of the range (31 world
  units), and the sunlit floor in front of Kurt shadowed itself (16,547 pixels
  darkened where the emulator darkens 3,062). Native writes DO land: 13,285
  texels, exactly the emulator ramp map's texels, depths equal to 1/65536 (and
  with the low byte the ramp cannot give).
So the renderer asks the GPU, and measures BOTH paths:
- testNativeShadowWrites, once, outside any frame, before the first frame that
  wants a shadow, into an 8x8 bare shadow target and 8x8 probes, read back:
  THE RAMP: one quad through the caster program and the ramp at a depth whose
  middle byte is 0x37; exact when all 64 texels carry it.
  NATIVE: sample the map behind a receiver (lit), write a quad natively, sample
  again (dark): it works when all 64 texels moved and all 64 receivers went dark.
  The decision: the ramp where it is exact (it is what the emulator's real pass
  needs); native where the ramp is not and native works; else the ramp, logged
  as NEITHER. A native session frees the ramp.
    console:  "the ramp does not work (0 of 64 texels carry the middle byte,
               first FF000080); native writes WORK (64 of 64 written ...; 64 of
               64 receivers behind them dark ...); the caster pass uses native
               writes"
    emulator: "the ramp WORKS (64 of 64 ..., first FF003780); native writes
               WORK (...); the caster pass uses the ramp"
  (The emulator's native writes pass this test and still land NOWHERE in the
  real 256x256 pass, which is why the ramp is preferred where it is exact, not
  native where it works.)
- a native session's first frames have their real map read back after
  C3D_FrameEnd: written texels -> verified; three frames in a row with casters
  drawn and nothing written -> the ramp, for good (the map released and remade
  with its depth buffer). Console: "native writes verified in the caster pass
  (186 casters, 6024 texels written)".
THE STALE COMBINER. The first version of the native test set the caster's
combiner through a TexEnv pointer fetched BEFORE the sampling draw; citro3d
marks a stage dirty only in C3D_GetTexEnv, so the edit after the draw was never
uploaded and the caster drew with the sampler's combiner (softness 0xFE on the
console). The pointer is fetched again after every draw that precedes an edit.
A native map's target has no depth buffer (the test measured a bare one; the
native writes keep the nearer depth themselves), which saves 128 KB of VRAM.

THE CASTER'S ALPHA. After the first independent review its alpha was made a
constant 255 (to keep the alpha test from dropping casters near the sun). In the
byte order the map uses, the alpha IS the depth's high byte, so every depth was
written as the far plane and there was no shadow at all: level 8 frame 60, the
shadow on and off, 0 pixels apart (the second review found it; the evidence
pictures were older than the change). The alpha is the ramp's again and the
caster pass runs with the alpha test OFF (casters whose textures have holes are
not drawn into the map at all); a pin with an arm now holds it.

5. MEMORY
---------
VRAM (6 MB): the map 512x512 RGBA8 (1.0 MB), or 256x256 (0.25 MB), plus, for the
ramp only, its 16-bit depth (0.5 / 0.125 MB). Linear heap: the 512x256 ramp
(0.5 MB, made in both modes). Every enhancement on at edge-smoothing scale 2:
the map leaves the glow its 768 KiB and is 256 ("1421312 free" native on the
console). GX queue: one entry a frame for the map's clear.

6. WHAT IT COSTS ON THE OLD 3DS
-------------------------------
Draws: level 8's opening has 219 opaque Lit draws; with the shadow on they are
drawn again into the map (no geometry shader) and again per eye by the receive
pass (geometry shader, depth-equal, one texture): 3x the lit geometry in mono, 5x
in stereo.
PROFILE (hw_cycle.sh --profile, make AUTOQUIT=90 PROFILE=1 LEVEL=8, every
enhancement on, edge smoothing 2x, glow 2 passes, native shadow map 256): Old 3DS
appliance, CFGU model 0, 268 MHz, L2 none, stereo ON, 957 frames in 90 s:
  frame period mean 94.1 ms, p50 <= 80.1 ms (steady state 76-81 ms, ~12.5 fps,
  every 60th-frame sample 76-113 ms); p95 is past the histogram's 100 ms top
  bucket; max 3,347 ms (one stall, not located; frames 2-4 at 243-250 ms are the
  start's level load settling);
  per frame: begin (the wait for the previous frame's GPU work) 26.8 ms, draw
  16.5 ms, end 26.6 ms, simulation 8.9 ms (5.4 steps of 1.65 ms).
  The game's audio drops ~1 of 4 queued buffers at this frame rate (ndsp
  "dropped=59" per 60 frames, dspdropped 0): an Enhanced-Mode-everything-on
  cost: the shorter-frame console runs today (33 ms and less) drop none.
The original look on the same console, level 7, stereo: ~21 ms; the light
alone ~24 ms (the toggle walk, 2026-11-18-3ds-console-hangs.txt section 4).

8. REVIEW AND VERIFICATION
--------------------------
Three independent read-only reviews (Fable), each of the code as it then
stood; nothing in them was built or run by the reviewer.
REVIEW 1 (the hang fixes + the first shadow pass): no blocker in the geometry
  rule, the GX budget, the glow re-tile or the allocator. HIGH: the caster's
  constant alpha wrote every depth as the far plane (section 2) -- FIXED, pinned.
  LOW: the receive pass lacked the whole-triangle guard -- FIXED. LOW: the split
  cap is per frame, not per eye -- OPEN (section 9 of the hangs file). LOW:
  casters/receivers drawn for draws the scene skipped -- OPEN, cosmetic.
REVIEW 2 (the native path, first version): no blocker. One empty frame
  demoted a console to the ramp for good -> three frames running (and now see
  review 3); the verified flag set before its allocation -> set after; native
  mode still allocated and required the ramp -> the ramp only when not native;
  a could-not-run test was sticky -> retried up to three times; the test's blend
  state inherited -> written; C3D_CLEAR_ALL on a depth-less target -> colour-only
  clear when native; stale comments -> rewritten. All seven FIXED.
REVIEW 3 (this design: both paths measured): no blocker, no hang, no GX
  overflow, no double free, no state leaking into the first real frame.
  - HIGH: three empty native frames demoted to the ramp -- the path the test had
    just measured broken on that device (native is chosen only there). FIXED:
    three empty frames settle the check and the map STAYS native; pinned
    (`native-map-demoted-to-the-broken-ramp`, `native-map-read-back-forever`).
  - MEDIUM: a readback out of a frame can be read before its copy finishes
    (C3D_SyncTextureCopy's PPF wait does not clear the event first; a frame's
    unobserved display transfer leaves it set). FIXED: every readback goes
    through readBack -- the destination pre-filled with the "did not work"
    answer and a trailing 64-byte copy into a renderer-owned drain, so the wait
    that returns is at the earliest the real copy's own; pinned
    (`native-map-read-back-without-the-drain`). The screenshot path's display
    transfer has the same exposure and is NOT changed (harness-only, and its
    captures have matched the console's own screenshots).
  - MEDIUM/LOW: a could-not-run try left a ramp-mode map (with a depth buffer)
    that a later native answer kept. FIXED: a native answer releases any map.
  - LOW: citro3d kept a pointer to the test's stack texture on unit 0. FIXED:
    unit 0 unbound before the texture goes.
  Re-measured after the fixes: section 4's console capture and the emulator's
  ramp capture (level 8 frame 60), every enhancement on, after the fixes:
    console (hw_cycle --capture, stereo): native chosen, "native writes
      verified in the caster pass (186 casters, 6024 texels written)", FRESH;
      the capture BYTE-IDENTICAL to the pre-fix one (md5 a47e6319...);
    emulator (compare_linux.sh 8 60, DISPLAY=:99, MDK_COMPARE_TOGGLES all on):
      the ramp chosen; the capture byte-identical to the pre-fix one.
Gates on the final tree: tests/scripts (make test), c3d_frame_test.sh, and
tools/check.sh (the R11 screen flagged the test's `v % kTestCorners`, a cold
one-shot loop; rewritten as two loops rather than pinned).

9. NOT DONE / NOT VERIFIED
--------------------------
- A caster's own texture: a surface whose texture has holes (a grating, a
  fence) casts NOTHING here, where the PC casts its cut-out.
- Blended Lit surfaces and the point lights' share of the light are not
  shadowed (the point lights are not drawn on the 3DS at all yet).
- The receive pass's edge fade is per vertex, and coplanar layered batches can
  be multiplied twice (depth-equal); both from the first review, open.
- The owner's New 3DS has not run shadows yet.
